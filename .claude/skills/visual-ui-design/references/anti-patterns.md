# Contextual Anti-Patterns

Use this registry to detect generic or unconsidered output. These are diagnostic signals, not universal bans. A pattern is acceptable when its semantics, content, platform, or brand context genuinely calls for it and the execution is coherent.

| Generic tell | Why it fails | Better response | When it may be valid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Card grid for every content type | Flattens hierarchy and fragments relationships | Let primary content live on the canvas; group only content that benefits from containment | Peer objects that users scan or compare as units |
| Sidebar plus header plus interchangeable stat cards | Copies a dashboard shell before understanding the work | Start from the dominant task and data relationship; earn persistent regions | Stable multi-area expert applications |
| Purple-blue glow or gradient as identity | Adds mood without product meaning | Derive atmosphere from brand, domain, content, or imagery | The brand or content genuinely owns that palette |
| Excessive pills, chips, and rounded rectangles | Turns every label into a control and erases semantic differences | Use plain text, aligned metadata, dividers, or real controls according to meaning | Tags, filters, statuses, and compact selectable values |
| Border + shadow + tint + radius on one element | Stacks decoration instead of establishing structure | Choose the minimum treatment that communicates containment or elevation | Deliberate skeuomorphic or highly expressive systems |
| Large empty hero with generic headline and two CTAs | Mimics a landing-page template without explaining value | Use product evidence, a meaningful demonstration, or a sharper editorial composition | A brand campaign where message and atmosphere are the content |
| Icon-only primary actions | Optimizes visual neatness at the cost of recognition | Pair labels with icons; reserve icon-only for familiar, repeated, space-constrained actions | Toolbars for expert users with strong conventions and accessible names |
| Identical radius on every nested object | Removes hierarchy and makes containment feel mechanically generated | Use a geometry system with optical nesting and role-based shapes | Intentionally uniform, flat systems without nested surfaces |
| One spacing value repeated everywhere | Makes unrelated and related content feel equally grouped | Use a small rhythm with tight, normal, and section-level relationships | Highly regular tabular or grid comparison |
| Font choice as the entire personality | Produces a fashionable shell with generic composition | Build voice through type relationships, content, spacing, and imagery | Type-led editorial or cultural products |
| Fake names, round metrics, lorem ipsum, and empty charts | Hides layout failure and makes the design impossible to judge | Use believable domain content, long and short cases, errors, and awkward data | Only a clearly labeled skeletal wireframe |
| Motion on every reveal and hover | Makes attention unpredictable and interaction feel slow | Choreograph a small motion vocabulary around state, orientation, and emphasis | Entertainment or storytelling surfaces where motion is central |
| Glass or blur used to imply “native” or “premium” | Confuses a technique with platform fit or craftsmanship | Start with real platform behavior, content, geometry, and contrast | Layered interfaces where depth and backdrop context are meaningful |
| Mobile as a scaled-down desktop canvas | Preserves modules but loses task priority and reach | Recompose around mobile priority, input, interruption, and state | Rare fixed-canvas tools with explicit zoom/pan behavior |
| iOS as rounded cards, blur, and SF Symbols | Copies surface signals without native behavior | Use real navigation, presentation, safe-area, type, state, and accessibility conventions | “iOS inspired” work that truthfully re-specifies behavior for its platform |

## Review Questions

- Could this interface be swapped into an unrelated product after changing the logo and accent color?
- Did the layout emerge from the content and task, or from a remembered template?
- Is every visual treatment doing a distinct job?
- What has been deliberately left out?
- Where is the product-specific decision?
- Does the exception repeat as a system, or is it a one-off flourish?

Correct the underlying hierarchy, content, or composition before adding decoration. Anti-slop is not a monochrome aesthetic; it is evidence of deliberate choices.
