# Taste and Calibration

Use this reference to turn product context into a deliberate visual language. Taste is the quality of the choice in context, not the popularity of the style.

## Start With a Design Read

Write one sentence:

> Reading this as [product/surface] for [audience and task], where the interface should feel [qualities] so that [outcome], while avoiding [anti-qualities].

Qualities must imply visible decisions. “Calm under pressure,” “precise and coachable,” “warm without feeling childish,” and “authoritative without bureaucracy” are useful. “Modern,” “clean,” and “premium” are incomplete until translated into observable implications.

When an upstream UX artifact includes an Experience Read, preserve it. Visual design may sharpen its expression but must not silently replace it.

## Calibrate Four Dials

Use these named anchors. Choose an anchor or a position between anchors; do not imply scientific precision.

| Dial | Low anchor | Middle anchor | High anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Familiarity | Established platform grammar | Familiar structure with one distinctive move | Unconventional composition that remains learnable |
| Density | Selective content and generous pauses | Balanced working density | Compact, comparison-rich, expert rhythm |
| Expression | Quiet, utilitarian, low decoration | Branded and recognizable | Art-directed, highly expressive, content-led |
| Motion | Still, immediate feedback only | Purposeful transitions and state changes | Choreographed motion central to the experience |

The dials answer different questions. A dense tool can still be expressive. A distinctive editorial surface can use almost no motion. Do not make one dial mechanically determine another.

## Form Candidate Theses

When the visual direction is open, form at least two short, coherent theses. Each thesis should specify:

- the intended attention order;
- composition and density logic;
- typographic voice;
- color and surface behavior;
- motion role;
- one signature move;
- one deliberate omission or restraint.

Compare candidates against high design pressures, platform fit, content, brand evidence, accessibility, feasibility, and the Design Read. Select the candidate with the strongest overall fit, not the loudest or newest aesthetic.

## Use References as Evidence of Decisions

When references are available, deconstruct them instead of imitating them. Record:

- what first captures attention;
- how the eye moves after that;
- how space groups and separates content;
- how type creates voice and information hierarchy;
- which colors are structural, semantic, or atmospheric;
- what is deliberately absent;
- which details signal craft;
- which qualities depend on that reference's content, brand, or platform.

Extract a principle, not a screenshot. Combine references by role: one for composition, one for type or color, one for interaction or craft. Avoid copying the whole visual identity of one product.

## Spend a Distinction Budget

Use one or two strong, repeatable choices rather than making every element unusual. A signature move may be:

- a product-specific content composition;
- a distinctive typographic relationship;
- a consistent image treatment;
- a domain-shaped data visualization;
- a recognizable state transition;
- a specific geometry or alignment rule.

The signature must reinforce the product's meaning or behavior. “Gradient,” “glass,” “big radius,” and “3D” are techniques, not a concept.

Define a restraint rule that protects the signature. Examples: only live-session states use the high-energy accent; only editorial moments break the grid; only one element per view receives deep elevation.

## Judge the Whole System

### Composition

Establish one focal point, a readable scan path, and quiet areas that let emphasis register. Avoid equal-weight modules unless comparison is the actual task. Use alignment and negative space to create tension and grouping before adding containers.

### Typography

Choose type for voice, readability, platform support, and content. Create contrast through role, scale, weight, width, case, and spacing. Do not rely on a fashionable font to provide personality. Check real labels, long names, metrics, localization, and text scaling.

### Color

Assign structural, semantic, interactive, and atmospheric roles. Saturation and contrast create attention; reserve them accordingly. A limited palette may be quiet or bold. A large palette can be coherent when role boundaries are disciplined.

### Spatial rhythm

Use repeated relationships, not isolated values. Spacing communicates grouping, pace, and density. Mix tight local rhythm with larger pauses. Uniform gaps everywhere flatten hierarchy as surely as uniform type sizes.

### Craft

Check optical alignment, nested geometry, icon weight, baseline consistency, image cropping, border joins, surface transitions, state continuity, and the quality of real content. Craft is where a coherent system stops feeling like a template.

## Distinguish Defaults From Rules

Accessibility, semantics, state clarity, responsive behavior, and platform truth are quality floors. Aesthetic guidance is contextual. Never convert current anti-slop fashion into a universal ban.

Break a default only with a reason tied to the Design Read. If the exception cannot be explained and repeated coherently, it is probably arbitrary.
