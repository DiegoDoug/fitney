# Adaptive contrast example

## Trackside coaching app

Evidence: coaches, repeated expert use, iPad, outdoor glare, video analysis, touch while standing.

High pressures: environmental readability, touch ergonomics, rapid recognition, persistent context.

Design Read: a field tool for coaches making rapid, repeated decisions outdoors; it should feel immediate, precise, and calm under pressure, not cinematic or delicate.

Calibration: established familiarity; compact density; quiet expression; purposeful motion.

Response: iPadOS adapted + utilitarian/data-dense; high contrast, large primary transport controls, compact secondary metadata, stable toolbars, restrained surfaces, strong selected/recording states, functional motion. Signature move: the live session state becomes a continuous high-contrast timing rail across video, rep, and athlete context. Restraint rule: only live/recording information receives the high-energy accent.

## Luxury marketplace

Evidence: consumer browsing, photography, brand perception, lower task density.

High pressures: content immersion, trust, brand expression.

Design Read: a consumer marketplace where photography and provenance must build desire and trust; it should feel curated and tactile, not sparse for its own sake or “luxury” by black-and-gold cliché.

Calibration: balanced familiarity; spacious density; branded expression; still-to-purposeful motion.

Response: web-native + content-first/editorial; image dominance, generous pacing, editorial typography, reduced chrome, detailed product metadata at decision points, subtle functional motion. Signature move: provenance details align directly with the image crops they explain. Restraint rule: product imagery supplies most color; interface chrome remains quiet.

The same skill yields different systems because the pressures differ.
